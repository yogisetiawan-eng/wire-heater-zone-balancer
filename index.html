How it calculates

Workflow. 1 · Estimate the test from physics before you build it. 2 · Run the test at full power and enter the measured probes; the estimate is shown beside each reading. 3 · Adjust: the new layout is tuned on the measured readings, not on the estimate.

Estimate. Each section loses heat by natural convection on a vertical surface (Churchill–Chu, with the strip's vertical extent as the length: width for top and bottom, length for left and right) plus radiation ε·σ·(Ts²+Ta²)(Ts+Ta), from the front face or both faces, times the extra loss factor. Sections share heat through the plate as below. Mounts and attached panels usually drain much more heat than the surface alone: step 2 shows the factor that would make the estimate match your readings, so you can use it for the next design.

Sections. Each side is split into three equal sections, one per probe, so a frame of four sides becomes twelve sections. Test power per section follows from the wiring: in one series string every section carries I = V / ΣRᵢ, so Pᵢ = I²·Ω/m·Lᵢ; with one string per side the current is V / (Ω/m·L_side).

Heat sharing. Neighbouring sections exchange heat through the plate: Kᵢⱼ = k·t·width / spacing, summed over the front and backing plates, with corner links when the sides form a closed frame. Each section's own loss to the room is then Gᵢ = (Pᵢ − Σ Kᵢⱼ·(Tᵢ − Tⱼ)) / (Tᵢ − T_room). This takes out the heat a section only passes along to its neighbours, which the simple one-probe method could not.

Balancing. Loss rises slightly faster than ΔT, modelled as Gᵢ·ΔTᵢ,test^(−n)·ΔT^(1+n). When every section sits at the same temperature no heat flows between them, so the graded layout gives each section Pᵢ = Gᵢ·ΔTᵢ,test^(−n)·ΔT^(1+n). That sets each side's wire total and how it is spread over its three sections. With uniform pitch each side's power is spread evenly, and the four side powers are chosen by least squares to flatten the temperature as far as possible.

Wire. In series R_total = V²/P and each section's wire is Lᵢ = R_total·(Pᵢ/P) / Ω/m; with one string per side, L_side = V² / (P_side·Ω/m). Pitch = section area ÷ wire length. Wire estimate = predicted SS + wire load × R′.

Prediction. Predicted temperatures come from solving the full heat balance, sharing included, with the new powers. Rebuild, measure again and press Use this layout for the next test to refine. Use full-on power for the test: if a controller was cycling, the real power was lower than calculated.
